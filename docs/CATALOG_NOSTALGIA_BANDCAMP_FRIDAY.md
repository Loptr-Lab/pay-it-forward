# Catalog, nostalgia, and Bandcamp Friday — implementation brief

**Editorial series:** The Catalog Is Not Dead · What an Artist Actually Buys From a Label · The Nostalgia Cycle  
**Campaign opportunity:** December 4, 2026 Bandcamp Friday  
**Status:** Research and editorial implementation only; no artist partnership, rights, financial, or campaign authorization is implied.

## Editorial thesis
Play It Forward should teach artists to treat fandom as a lifecycle: discovery → connection → memory → dormancy → reactivation → renewed relationship. Catalog activity may be reactivated by cultural moments, tours, anniversaries, collaborations, and fan storytelling, but uplift is a hypothesis to measure—not a guaranteed outcome.

## NOT A TOY research/campaign gates
- Confirm master, publishing, artwork, video, merch, and direct-sale rights, including label restrictions.
- Obtain artist/management approval before describing plans or publishing campaign materials as artist-facing.
- Review actual statements, contract terms, recoupment, label deductions, service scope, and any release/option/exclusivity obligations with qualified counsel and accountant.
- Model label services against independently priced replacements, including distribution, accounting, marketing, licensing, legal, fulfillment, fixed costs, processing, workload, and risk.
- Keep creative autonomy as a separate qualitative decision; the financial model describes costs and trade-offs, not the artist's value judgment.
- No Bandcamp catalog bundle, discount, exclusive, or campaign claim until ownership/control and permissions are verified.

## Bandcamp Friday
Bandcamp's official Help Center lists December 4, 2026 as the final 2026 date. Bandcamp waives its revenue-share fee during the event; payment processing fees still apply. The event is midnight-to-midnight Pacific. Check the official page before publishing time-sensitive copy.

## Proposed audience experiment (only if artist-authorized)
1. Before: ask fans to voluntarily share the song/release/moment they remember; no forced posting or data collection.
2. Day-of: point to authorized direct catalog pages; tell fans what the purchase supports only if the artist can substantiate it.
3. Offer: a simple curated catalog path or bundle, only for rights-cleared items; no artificial scarcity.
4. After: thank buyers, review net sales and page traffic against a defined baseline, capture qualitative responses with consent, and document artist workload.
5. Continue: use the results to inform future creative/community activity; do not turn fans into unpaid promotional labor.
